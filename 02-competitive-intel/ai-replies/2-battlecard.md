## GPT-6

### Competitive Battlecard: GoHen

Roamly Groups | Internal Product & GTM Strategy | V1

Competitive threat: VERY HIGH

Primary battleground: Hen parties, stag parties and milestone celebrations.

Competitive positioning: GoHen sells stress-free occasion planning. Roamly sells curated local experiences with group coordination built into the booking.

### Executive takeaway

GoHen is a more direct competitor than a conventional experience marketplace. It already solves much of the organiser’s pain: individual payments, guest invitations, payment reminders and shared itineraries.

Do not compete with GoHen on split payments or group administration alone. It already has credible capabilities there. Compete on experience discovery, flexibility and the quality of the local experience.

⸻

### 1. KNOW THE LANDSCAPE

#### Competitor overview

**GoHen**

GoHen is a specialist hen-party planning platform, established in 2002 and now operating under Funktion Leisure following its acquisition of the GoHen brand in July 2026.

Its proposition is to remove the administrative burden of organising group celebrations by combining activities, accommodation, packages and individual payments.

Its website advertises:

- 750,000+ guests served.
- 4.8/5 Trustpilot rating.
- More than 80 destinations.
- More than 1,000 activities.
- Dedicated event planners.

These are significant competitive advantages in its core market.

#### Key offerings

| Offering | What it does | Competitive implication |
|---|---|---|
| Group packages | Combines accommodation, activities and nightlife. | Stronger proposition for complete weekend planning. |
| VIP Area | Guest lists, invitations and payment tracking. | Direct overlap with Roamly Groups. |
| Individual payments | Guests pay their own deposits and balances. | Removes one of Roamly’s proposed differentiators. |
| Payment reminders | Automated reminders to guests. | Reduces organiser administration. |
| Shared itinerary | Centralised event information. | Direct overlap with shared planning. |
| Dedicated planners | Human assistance with quotations and arrangements. | Adds reassurance and service beyond software. |

GoHen’s VIP Area is particularly important: guests can register independently, pay deposits, make instalments and see payment status.

#### Pricing

GoHen uses package-based, per-person pricing, generally provided through a quotation.

Publicly advertised examples include:

| Product | Advertised price |
|---|---|
| Liverpool hen packages | From £59pp |
| Bath hen packages | From £69pp |
| HenFest | From £229pp |
| Standard initial deposit | £50 |
| Promotional zero-deposit offer | £0, subject to conditions |

Pricing varies by destination, date, accommodation and activities. The zero-deposit promotion is conditional, and subsequent guest deposits still apply.

Roamly implication: Do not assume Roamly is cheaper. Compete on the flexibility and relevance of individual experiences, not an unsupported price advantage.

⸻

### 2. UNDERSTAND THE BATTLEFIELD

#### Key strengths: Where GoHen wins

| Strength | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Specialist occasion expertise | Understands the specific requirements of hen parties. |
| Established reputation | Significant customer history and visible social proof. |
| End-to-end packages | Organiser can arrange multiple parts of a weekend together. |
| Individual payments | Already solves the money-collection problem. |
| Human support | Dedicated planners help customers who don’t want self-service. |
| Established supplier network | Broad activity and destination coverage. |

The biggest threat: GoHen has already productised the exact administrative problem Roamly Groups is intended to solve.

It would be commercially naive to position split payments as a unique selling point.

#### Key weaknesses: Where GoHen is vulnerable

| Weakness | Roamly opportunity |
|---|---|
| Occasion-specific positioning | Serve birthdays, friend groups and other social occasions without requiring a traditional hen-party package. |
| Package-oriented purchasing | Make individual experiences easier to discover and book. |
| Planning-led journey | Offer a more immediate, self-service booking experience. |
| Organiser-centric service | Make the experience useful to every guest, not just the organiser. |
| Broad package choices | Differentiate through curated local experts and trusted experiences. |

One qualification: these are strategic vulnerabilities, not proven product deficiencies. GoHen also offers individual activities and customisable packages.

⸻

### 3. WIN THE DEAL

#### Why Roamly wins

Roamly’s strongest proposition is not better group administration. It is better experience discovery combined with group administration.

Three defensible advantages:

**1. Curated local expertise**

Roamly connects travellers with vetted local experts across 40 cities.

The host network and quality standards provide a foundation for differentiation beyond generic activity aggregation.

**2. Flexible experience discovery**

Customers can start with an individual experience rather than committing to a complete weekend package.

This suits organisers who want one memorable activity without outsourcing the entire event.

**3. Integrated group experience**

Roamly Groups combines discovery, coordinated booking, individual payments and shared itineraries.

The opportunity is to make the entire journey feel native to the experience marketplace.

Importantly, the defensibility comes from the combination of trusted supply, marketplace liquidity and customer experience, not simply the payment functionality.

#### Why GoHen wins

Be honest with the sales team:

GoHen is likely to win when:

- The customer wants an entire hen weekend organised.
- The organiser values human assistance.
- The group wants accommodation, nightlife and several activities together.
- The customer already trusts GoHen.
- The group needs flexible deposits and instalments.

If the buyer wants a complete hen-party package, GoHen has the stronger proposition today.

Roamly should not force that comparison.

#### Why You: Roamly’s positioning

| GoHen | Roamly Groups |
|---|---|
| Organise your hen weekend | Discover an experience worth sharing |
| Package-led | Experience-led |
| Specialist occasion planning | Flexible group experiences |
| Dedicated event planners | Self-service group booking |
| Group administration | Shared discovery, booking and planning |

#### Suggested positioning statement

GoHen helps you organise a hen weekend. Roamly helps your group discover and book exceptional local experiences together. Everyone gets involved, everyone pays their share, and the whole experience stays in one place.

### 4. HANDLE THE HARD MOMENTS

#### Quick dismiss

Use these responses to redirect conversations without dismissing genuine competitive strengths.

| Prospect says | Suggested response |
|---|---|
| “GoHen already lets everyone pay individually.” | “Absolutely. Where Roamly differs is that group payments are integrated into a curated local-experience marketplace.” |
| “GoHen can organise the whole weekend.” | “That’s a strength. Roamly is particularly useful when you’re looking for one exceptional experience rather than outsourcing the entire weekend.” |
| “They have more activities.” | “They have considerable breadth. Our focus is on curated experiences and vetted local experts.” |
| “They have dedicated planners.” | “That’s valuable if you want someone to organise everything. Roamly offers a more self-service way to discover and book together.” |
| “Why should we change?” | “If your existing process works, there’s no reason to change. But if finding the right experience and coordinating the group is difficult, that’s where we’d like to help.” |

#### Landmine questions

These are questions Roamly should ask prospects to uncover where its proposition has an advantage.

| Question | What you’re testing |
|---|---|
| “Are you looking for an entire weekend package or just one exceptional experience?” | Package versus individual experience demand. |
| “How much of the planning do you want someone else to handle?” | Human-service preference versus self-service. |
| “Do all your guests have to agree before you can secure a booking?” | Group commitment friction. |
| “How do you currently discover experiences that everyone will enjoy?” | Discovery and curation gaps. |
| “Would you prefer a single group package or the flexibility for everyone to participate and pay individually?” | Purchasing flexibility. |
| “Would you use the same platform for birthdays, holidays and other occasions?” | Potential for repeat usage beyond hen and stag events. |

Best landmine question:

“If you’ve already solved collecting everyone’s money, what’s still the hardest part of organising the experience?”

This shifts the discussion away from a feature comparison and towards the underlying customer problem.

#### Objection handling

**Objection 1: “GoHen already does everything Roamly Groups does.”**

Response:

“GoHen has a strong proposition for organising hen weekends, including individual payments and shared planning. Roamly approaches the problem from a different starting point: discovering exceptional local experiences and making it easy for a group to book them together. We’re not trying to replace every event-planning service.”

**Objection 2: “GoHen has more choice.”**

Response:

“They have extensive activity and package coverage. Roamly focuses on curated experiences delivered by vetted local experts. The question isn’t simply how many activities are available, but whether you can find something your group genuinely wants to do.”

**Objection 3: “Why wouldn’t we use GoHen?”**

Response:

“If you want a complete hen weekend, GoHen is a credible option. If you’re looking for a particular local experience and want everyone to coordinate and pay through one booking, Roamly is designed for that.”

**Objection 4: “Roamly is just another booking platform.”**

Response:

“The difference is the group journey. Instead of the organiser discovering, booking and collecting money separately, Roamly brings discovery, individual payments and shared planning together.”

### 5. CPO VERDICT: WHERE TO COMPETE

| Dimension | Winner | Roamly strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Hen-party specialisation | GoHen | Don’t compete head-on. |
| Complete weekend packages | GoHen | Avoid package-led positioning. |
| Individual payments | GoHen currently has an established proposition | Match the expected capability; don’t claim uniqueness. |
| Local experience curation | Roamly’s potential advantage | Lead with vetted local experts. |
| Flexible experience discovery | Roamly’s potential advantage | Make individual experiences the entry point. |
| Group booking experience | Both have credible propositions | Differentiate through usability and conversion. |
| Broader social occasions | Roamly’s potential opportunity | Expand beyond hen and stag groups. |

#### Three actions I’d prioritise

1. Product: Benchmark the complete GoHen VIP journey against Roamly Groups, particularly invitations, payment tracking, reminders, guest defaults and cancellations.
2. GTM: Position Roamly around curated local experiences rather than split payments. Test this with existing customers exhibiting multi-account booking behaviour.
3. Measurement: Compare completed group bookings, organiser effort and subsequent independent guest bookings. Demonstrate an advantage rather than assume one.

⸻

### Final competitive assessment

GoHen is a direct competitor, not an outdated incumbent waiting to be disrupted.

It has a strong brand, established supplier relationships, individual payment functionality and a specialist understanding of occasion planning.

Roamly’s opportunity is to own a different customer need:

GoHen helps organise the occasion. Roamly makes it easier to discover and book something exceptional together.

That is a credible positioning, provided the product delivers a genuinely better group booking experience.

Research note: Competitor capabilities and advertised pricing are based on GoHen’s publicly available materials reviewed on 5 November 2026. Roamly advantages are strategic hypotheses to validate through customer research and launch data, not independently established comparative performance.

## Claude

### Battlecard: Roamly Groups vs GoHen

Nov 5, 2026 · Martin

GoHen already sells our core promise, individual payments with no chasing, to our exact target organiser. We win on curated local experiences booked self-serve in minutes, not on split payments.

### Know the landscape

#### Competitor overview

- **Who:** UK-based hen party planner, trading since 2002, operating on the Groupia platform. Claims over 700k clients.
- **Buyer:** the maid of honour or bridesmaid organising a hen weekend for a group.
- **Coverage:** 70+ hen destinations across the UK and Europe.
- **Model:** packaged trips (accommodation, activities, nightlife), quoted and supported by a dedicated event planner and phone team.
- **Positioning:** one booking, everyone pays individually, no chasing. Bonded and protected, so the organiser is not at risk.

#### Key offerings

| Offering | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hen packages | Accommodation, activities and nightlife bundled into itineraries |
| Individual payments | Each guest pays their own deposit and instalments online |
| Groupia app | Itinerary on the go, tracks who has paid, sends notifications |
| Pay for someone else | Any guest can cover the bride's share or another guest's |
| Dedicated event planner | Human help by phone and WhatsApp |
| AI hen planner | Chat-based planning assistant (new) |
| Financial protection | ABTA and ABTOT bonded |

#### Pricing

| Element | GoHen | Roamly Groups |
|---|---|---|
| Booking deposit | Small initial deposit from the organiser (standard £50) | TBC |
| Guest deposits | Each guest pays a deposit (standard £50pp) within about 1 month | TBC (set by unpaid-share rule) |
| Final balance | Due about 6 weeks before the event, with final numbers | TBC |
| Unpaid places | No longer guaranteed after the guest deposit deadline | Organiser covers or releases seats (proposed) |
| Package price | Per-person quote; not captured in this research | Per-person experience price |

Terms vary by itinerary and promotion, including zero-deposit offers. Check live terms before quoting them to prospects.

### Understand the battlefield

#### Key strengths

- **They own the promise we're launching with.** Individual payments, payment tracking and "no chasing" are live, proven and central to their reviews.
- **Their non-payer rule is solved and familiar.** Guest deposits within about a month, unpaid places released, final numbers about 6 weeks out. Organisers trust it because it works.
- **Financial protection.** ABTOT bonding lets them frame solo planning as risky. We have no equivalent badge.
- **Whole-trip scope.** Accommodation, activities and nightlife in one booking. We sell the experience only.
- **Human support.** A dedicated planner reassures a nervous first-time organiser.
- **Scale and social proof.** 20+ years and hundreds of thousands of clients in this exact segment.
- **Deposit-led pricing.** A small deposit secures the booking, which lowers the barrier to committing early.

#### Key weaknesses

- **Slow start.** A quote-first, enquiry-led flow sits between the organiser and a booking. It is not self-serve in minutes.
- **Generic, party-led inventory.** Built for party destinations (boat parties, cocktail classes, bar crawls), not distinctive local experiences.
- **Hen and stag only.** Little fit for milestone birthdays, mixed-gender groups or a single experience rather than a weekend.
- **Package lock-in.** The value is in the bundle. An organiser who already has accommodation, or wants one great experience, gets less from them.
- **No life beyond the event.** Guests have little reason to return once the hen is over. Their model is episodic.
- **Payment rules can feel rigid.** For example, the bride's cost can't be spread across everyone's price. Guests have to pay on her behalf.

*Assessment note:* the weaknesses are our reading of their model from public pages, not verified customer complaints. Validate them in win/loss interviews.

### Win the deal

#### Why you win

| Where we win | Why it's defensible |
|---|---|
| **Distinctive local experiences** from vetted hosts in 40 cities | The moat is supply. Recruiting and vetting local experts city by city takes years and is costly for a package aggregator to replicate. |
| **Self-serve in minutes**, no quote or phone call | Product-led flow on a platform users already trust (repeat rate 3x industry). Defensible only while we keep speed and reliability ahead. |
| **Occasions beyond hen and stag**: milestone birthdays, mixed groups, a single experience | Our catalogue isn't party-specific, so the segment is wider than theirs by design. |
| **Guests become customers** | Every guest already sees Roamly and can book their own trip later. GoHen's audience leaves after the event. Compounds only if the guest loop is proven. |
| **Bolt-on to a trip already booked** | The organiser keeps their own accommodation and adds one great experience. GoHen's model needs the bundle. |

#### Why they win

Be honest about these. We lose these deals today.

| Where they win | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| **Whole hen weekend in one booking** | If the organiser wants accommodation plus nightlife plus activities, we lose. Don't fight it: position Roamly for the hero experience. |
| **Financial protection (ABTOT)** | A nervous organiser picks the bonded option. We need a clear, visible guarantee on payments and cancellations. |
| **Proven payment rules** | They've solved who pays when a guest doesn't. Until our rule is live and clear, they win on certainty. |
| **A human planner** | First-time organisers who want hand-holding will pick them. We win the confident self-serve organiser. |
| **Brand in the category** | "Hen do" search and word of mouth lead to them. We are unknown in this category. |

#### Why you

**For organisers who want a group experience worth remembering, not a party package:** Roamly lets you book one standout experience with a vetted local host for the whole group in minutes. Everyone pays their own share and sees the same plan, so you never chase money. If someone doesn't pay by the deadline, you decide whether to cover their seat or release it.

### Handle the hard moments

#### Quick dismiss

"GoHen is great if you want a hen package. If you want one experience your group will actually remember, booked in minutes with everyone paying their own share, that's Roamly."

#### Landmine questions

Ask these to surface where GoHen is a weak fit, without attacking them:

1. "Have you already sorted the accommodation, or do you need it as part of the booking?" (Already sorted points to Roamly.)
2. "Do you want to book it today, or wait for a quote?" (Today points to Roamly.)
3. "Is this a typical hen activity, or something the group couldn't do anywhere else?" (Something unique points to Roamly.)
4. "Is everyone coming for the whole weekend, or just for the main event?" (Just the event points to Roamly.)
5. "Is it a hen or stag, or another occasion?" (Another occasion points to Roamly.)

**Don't set landmines on:** payment certainty, financial protection or human support. They win those today.

#### Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "GoHen already lets everyone pay individually." | "True, and so do we. The difference is what you're booking: a vetted local host's experience, self-serve in minutes, not a quoted package." |
| "GoHen is ABTOT protected. Are you?" | Answer honestly with Roamly's actual payment and cancellation protection. Never imply bonding we don't have. If the gap matters to the organiser, this is a GoHen deal. |
| "What if someone doesn't pay?" | "Every guest has a deadline to pay their share. At the deadline, you choose: cover the gap or release the seat. The host gets confirmed numbers either way." |
| "I want accommodation, activities and nightlife in one place." | "Then a package planner may suit you better for the weekend. Book Roamly for the one experience that makes it special." Don't overreach. |
| "I'd rather talk to a person." | Point to Roamly support for group bookings. If the organiser needs a dedicated planner, concede it. |
| "Their deposits are smaller." | Compare like for like: the total cost per guest, and what's included. Lead with value per head, not the deposit. |

**Open item:** the protection and non-payer answers depend on the unpaid-share rule being signed off. Update this card when it is.
