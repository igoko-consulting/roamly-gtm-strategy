## GPT-6

### Roamly Groups: Competitive Landscape

### Executive assessment

Roamly is entering a market where group booking and split payments are not new problems, and some competitors already solve both particularly well.

The opportunity is not simply to let everyone pay their share. It is to combine:

- Curated, trusted local experiences.
- Simple group coordination and decision-making.
- Individual payments within one booking.
- A shared itinerary.
- Discovery across multiple occasions, not just hen and stag weekends.

I’d focus on three competitors: one particularly strong specialist, one established group-event operator and one major experience marketplace.

### 1. Top three competitors

#### 1. GoHen / StagWeb

Competitive threat: Very high — particularly for occasion groups.

An important competitor you might not initially have considered if you’re thinking primarily about experience marketplaces.

These are specialist brands now operating under Funktion Leisure, following their acquisition in 2026. GoHen focuses on hen parties; StagWeb on stag weekends.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Primary customer | Hen and stag organisers planning group celebrations. |
| Positioning | Stress-free group planning, curated activities, packages and individual payments. |
| Where they win | Their VIP Area allows organisers to invite guests, track payments, manage instalments and access shared itineraries. Guests pay individually rather than relying on the organiser to collect everything. |
| Where vulnerable | Strongly occasion-specific and package-oriented. Less naturally positioned around spontaneous discovery of individual local experiences across multiple customer segments. |

Why they matter: They already address almost exactly the pain in your value proposition. GoHen explicitly markets itself around eliminating the need to chase guests for money.

Roamly’s opportunity: Offer the same organisational convenience without requiring customers to plan an entire hen or stag weekend. Make individual experiences the starting point.

One additional consideration: their recent ownership transition makes continuity and customer confidence worth monitoring, although it doesn’t establish a weakness in their current service.

Official sites: GoHen · StagWeb

⸻

#### 2. The Stag Company

Competitive threat: High — particularly for stag groups.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Primary customer | Stag organisers planning weekends and group activities. |
| Positioning | Experienced event planners who take the hassle out of organising a stag weekend. |
| Where they win | Dedicated event planners, established supplier relationships, flexible payments, individual guest deposits and an online Party Hub. |
| Where vulnerable | Primarily focused on stag celebrations and multi-activity packages. Less relevant to broader social occasions and everyday local experience discovery. |

The particularly interesting feature is its Party Hub.

Organisers can invite guests, track payments and manage the booking. Guests pay individually, with flexible instalments. It also provides a shared itinerary.

Roamly’s opportunity: Deliver comparable group convenience through a more flexible, self-service experience marketplace, rather than an event-planning service.

Official site: The Stag Company

⸻

#### 3. Airbnb Experiences

Competitive threat: High — particularly for experience discovery.

This is the broader marketplace competitor.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Primary customer | Travellers and locals looking for distinctive experiences hosted by local experts. |
| Positioning | Discover authentic experiences through local hosts, with the option to book privately as a group. |
| Where they win | Brand recognition, marketplace reach, reviews, host discovery and private group reservations. |
| Where vulnerable | Private group availability depends on host discretion. Its published booking flow focuses on reserving group places, rather than providing an explicit individual payment-collection workflow. |

Airbnb supports private group experiences, group pricing and invitations. Reservations also generate group messaging between guests and hosts. Hosts can set a minimum price for private groups.

Roamly’s opportunity: Differentiate through an integrated group-first booking experience, particularly individual payments, coordinated commitment and shared planning.

Official site: Airbnb Experiences

### 3. The status quo: What happens if the organiser chooses none?

The real competitor is often WhatsApp, spreadsheets and manual payments.

This is arguably the most important competitor because it is what your target customer already uses.

A typical journey looks like this:

| Step | Current behaviour | Friction |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Organiser searches Google, Instagram and experience platforms. | Too many options to coordinate. |
| Decide | WhatsApp group chat and polls. | Opinions, availability and preferences become difficult to reconcile. |
| Book | Organiser books for everyone using one account. | Financial exposure and uncertainty about attendance. |
| Collect | Bank transfers, Revolut or cash. | Chasing payments and reconciling who owes what. |
| Coordinate | WhatsApp messages and shared Google Sheets. | Information gets fragmented and people miss updates. |
| Experience | Organiser shares booking confirmations and itinerary manually. | No single source of truth. |

Other tools, such as Splitwise, help reconcile group expenses but don’t solve experience discovery, availability or booking.

The status quo wins because it is familiar, flexible and effectively free. Its weakness is the administrative burden placed on the organiser.

### 4. My strategic recommendation for Roamly

I’d sharpen your competitive positioning around three differentiators.

| Differentiator | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Curated local experiences | Compete on the quality and trustworthiness of the experience, not simply the payment mechanism. |
| Group-first booking | Remove the organiser’s administrative burden through invitations, coordinated commitment and individual payments. |
| One shared experience | Keep the booking, payments and itinerary together, creating value for the entire group. |

#### Suggested positioning

Roamly Groups makes organising memorable experiences effortless. Discover something worth doing, bring everyone together, and let each person pay their share. One booking, one shared plan, no chasing.

#### One important CPO observation

I’d challenge one aspect of your original value proposition.

“Everyone pays their own share” is a feature, not necessarily a defensible differentiator.

GoHen and The Stag Company already offer this.

Your stronger proposition is:

The easiest way to discover, agree and book exceptional local experiences together.

Payment is one part of that experience.

That distinction also supports your existing GTM strategy:

- Expand: Convert existing Roamly users who already demonstrate multi-account booking behaviour.
- Prove: Demonstrate that coordinated discovery, commitment and payment increase completed bookings.
- Acquire: Turn invited guests into independent Roamly customers.

The competitive opportunity is to own the space between traditional experience marketplaces and specialist group-event planners.

My verdict: Don’t try to become another GoHen or another Airbnb. Become the best place to organise and book a great local experience with friends.

## Claude

### Roamly Groups: top three competitors for occasion organisers

Bottom line: your value proposition is not new in the hen and stag market. Specialist planners already sell "everyone pays individually". Roamly's edge has to come from curated local hosts and self-serve speed, not from split payments alone.

### 1. GoHen / Groupia (specialist hen and stag planners): the one you may not have considered

- Who they serve: hen and stag organisers, usually the maid of honour or best man, booking packages of accommodation, activities and nightlife. They offer over 70 hen destinations across the UK and Europe.
- Positioning: essentially your value proposition, already live. They promise one booking with no chasing for money, and individual payments so the organiser isn't out of pocket. Their app lets the organiser track who has paid, and they claim to be the only hen company with one.
- Where they win:
  - A solved non-payer rule. Guests typically get about a month to pay an individual deposit, after which unpaid places are no longer guaranteed, with final numbers due around six weeks before the event. This is the rule you still need to set.
  - Trust signals. They are ABTOT bonded and market against planning solo, which they say saves 13 hours on average.
  - Scale and proof. They cite over 700k clients since 2002.
- Where they're most vulnerable (my assessment):
  - Package and enquiry led. You request a custom quote first, so it is not self-serve in minutes.
  - Generic party inventory. It is built for party destinations, not curated local experiences.
  - Narrow fit. The model suits hen and stag weekends, not a milestone birthday dinner-and-experience in 40 cities.
- Implication for Roamly: split payment is table stakes in this segment. Your differentiator is curated local hosts, booked self-serve in minutes, with the same payment certainty.

### 2. Airbnb Experiences

- Who they serve: Airbnb's existing stay guests, increasingly as a whole-trip app.
- Positioning: everything for the trip in one app, with group planning built in.
  - It has a new shared itinerary feature so groups can plan together.
  - Private Experiences can take groups of up to 30.
  - From an August 2026 partnership, Tripadvisor Group experiences will be bookable on Airbnb, drawing on a catalogue of over 425,000.
- Where they win:
  - Distribution and existing trust, plus attaching experiences to a stay the group has already booked.
  - Rapidly expanding supply.
- Where they're most vulnerable:
  - One payer for private groups. In private group bookings, hosts can set a minimum price and the group pays whichever is higher, but the flow still assumes one person confirms and pays. I found no current evidence of per-guest split payment for Experiences; worth verifying hands-on.
  - Diluted curation. Mass aggregated supply weakens a "vetted local expert" story.
  - Occasion organisers aren't their focus.

### 3. GetYourGuide (and Viator)

- Who they serve: independent travellers booking tours, tickets and activities, mainly one booker at a time.
- Positioning: the largest choice of things to do, with flexible booking. They are also marketing directly at your organiser. Their "Holiday Hero" campaign is built on the idea that every trip has one person who creates the group chat, books the experiences and keeps everyone on track.
- Where they win:
  - Breadth, SEO and paid reach.
  - Low-risk booking. Most private and small-group listings offer free cancellation up to 24 hours before. Reserve Now Pay Later lets travellers hold a booking without paying until closer to the date.
  - Distribution partnerships (Viator). Viator offers are integrated into Uber and Booking.com, and it became the first travel-experiences connected app in Google Gemini in July 2026.
- Where they're most vulnerable:
  - The organiser still fronts the cost. They have named the organiser's pain but, as far as I can see, still put one person on the hook for payment.
  - Commoditised inventory with less host vetting.
  - Weak group coordination beyond the booking itself.
- Implication for Roamly: they could copy your feature quickly. Speed to own the organiser's trust matters.

### Status quo: what the organiser does if they choose none of these

- The workaround:
  - The organiser books direct with a venue or on any platform using their own card.
  - Coordination happens in a WhatsApp group.
  - Money is chased through bank transfers, Monzo or Revolut requests, or Splitwise, and tracked in a spreadsheet or notes.
- Why it persists: it's free, flexible, needs no new account, and the organiser keeps control.
- What it costs them:
  - The organiser carries all the financial risk and the chasing.
  - Dropouts after booking are their loss.
  - Reviewers describe the relief of not having a bridesmaid pay for everyone and then chase repayment.
- Why this is your real competitor: most of your drop-off is probably losing to this, not to GoHen or Airbnb. The message to beat it is "keep your plan, lose the risk": shares are due by a deadline, and unpaid seats are released, not paid for by the organiser.

### So what for the GTM

- Positioning: split payment is necessary but not sufficient. Lead with curated local hosts, booked for the whole group in minutes, with no chasing.
- Price check: benchmark the deposit structure against GoHen's per-guest deposits. Organisers will compare.
- Watch list: monitor Airbnb's group features and its Tripadvisor supply rollout. That is the most credible fast-follower threat.
