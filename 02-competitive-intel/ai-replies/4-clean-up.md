### Competitive Battlecard: GoHen vs Roamly Groups

Competitor: GoHen

Category: Hen-party planning and group experiences

Competitive threat: Very high

Roamly target segment: Occasion groups of 6–12

Primary buyer: The group organiser

Strategic objective: Win bookings through better discovery and access to curated local experiences, not by claiming payment functionality GoHen already provides.

⸻

### 1. Know the Landscape

#### Competitor overview

GoHen is an established specialist in hen-party planning, offering activities, accommodation, packages and destination options across the UK and Europe.

Its proposition is built around taking the pressure off the organiser: bring the group together, choose a package and manage the arrangements in one place.

- Established: Operating since 2002.
- Scale: GoHen states that it has served more than 750,000 guests.
- Reputation: Displays a 4.8/5 Trustpilot rating.
- Coverage: Packages across the UK and Europe.
- Offering: Activities, accommodation, complete packages and individual experiences.
- Group functionality: Online VIP Area for guest registration, individual payments, instalments, reminders and itinerary management.

Sources: GoHen’s website and FAQs.

#### Ownership and accreditation

- Groupia Ltd entered administration on 16 June 2026.
- Funktion Leisure acquired GoHen and StagWeb out of administration in July 2026.
- Both brands are trading again under the new ownership.
- GoHen states that bookings have ABTA protection. Do not confuse this with ABTOT, which relates to arrangements for certain bookings made with the previous Groupia operator.

Sources: Funktion Leisure acquisition announcement and GoHen’s ownership update.

#### Pricing and payment model

| Item | Published information |
|---|---|
| Liverpool packages | From £59 per person |
| Bath packages | From £69 per person |
| HenFest | From £229 per person |
| Organiser deposit | Homepage displays £25 |
| Guest payments | Guests pay their own deposit within four weeks |
| Payment management | Individual payments, instalments and reminders through the VIP Area |

Qualification: The homepage advertises a £25 deposit, while published payment terms can vary by package and promotion. Do not present a £50 deposit as a universal requirement. The consequences for an individual guest who does not pay should not be assumed; confirm the applicable booking terms before making a claim.

Sources: GoHen homepage, package examples and payment FAQs.

### 2. Understand the Battlefield

#### What GoHen does well

| Strength | Competitive advantage |
|---|---|
| Occasion specialisation | Understands hen-party planning and group expectations. |
| Established reputation | Long trading history and substantial customer base. |
| Complete packages | Combines accommodation, activities and nightlife. |
| Individual payments | Guests can pay their own share rather than relying on the organiser to collect everything. |
| Planning support | Dedicated event managers provide human assistance. |
| Supplier network | Access to activities and suppliers suited to hen and stag groups. |
| Destination coverage | Established presence in popular UK and European destinations. |

#### Where Roamly could win

| Opportunity | Roamly’s potential advantage |
|---|---|
| Experience discovery | Curated local experiences rather than primarily package-led planning. |
| Local expertise | Vetted hosts and distinctive experiences can offer something beyond standard occasion activities. |
| Broader occasions | Potential to serve birthdays, celebrations and friend groups beyond hen and stag weekends. |
| Self-service booking | A simpler, more direct experience for groups wanting one excellent activity rather than a complete weekend package. |
| Existing marketplace | Roamly can potentially activate existing supply and customer relationships to generate group bookings. |

Important: These are potential advantages, not proven competitive wins. Roamly must demonstrate that its hosts and experiences are suitable for occasion groups and available in the destinations customers want.

#### Why they win

**1. Occasion-specific expertise**

GoHen is built around hen-party planning. Its proposition, suppliers and packages are designed for this audience.

**2. Host and supplier fit**

GoHen’s suppliers are selected to serve hen and stag groups.

Roamly’s curated hosts — such as food tours, workshops and other local experiences — may not want to accommodate a loud group of 12 or may not be equipped to serve occasion bookings.

If suitable hosts opt out, Roamly’s curated-experience advantage disappears for this segment.

**3. Destination coverage**

Hen weekends cluster around established UK party destinations such as Liverpool, Newcastle, Bath and Brighton, alongside a smaller number of popular European cities.

Roamly’s presence across 40 cities does not guarantee coverage of the destinations that matter most to this segment. Where relevant local supply is absent, there is no deal to win.

**4. Complete planning proposition**

GoHen can combine activities, accommodation and nightlife into a single package. Roamly should not assume it can compete equally well when customers want an entire weekend organised.

#### Why Roamly could win

Roamly should focus on the group that wants to discover and book one distinctive local experience together, rather than organise an entire weekend through a specialist planner.

Its potential differentiation is the combination of:

- Curated, vetted local experiences.
- Discovery across a broader range of experience types.
- Group booking and shared planning.
- A straightforward way for everyone to pay their share.

The distinction: GoHen helps organise the occasion. Roamly should make it easier to discover and book something exceptional together.

### 3. Win the Deal

#### Positioning

GoHen helps you organise a hen weekend. Roamly helps your group discover and book exceptional local experiences together. Everyone gets involved, everyone pays their share, and the whole experience stays in one place.

#### Why you win

Use Roamly when the customer:

- Wants one distinctive experience rather than a complete weekend package.
- Values curated local experiences and knowledgeable hosts.
- Wants a simple group booking and shared itinerary.
- Is organising a birthday, celebration or other group occasion beyond a traditional hen or stag weekend.
- Can find a suitable experience in their chosen destination.

#### Why they win

Be candid when the customer:

- Wants accommodation, activities and nightlife bundled together.
- Values specialist hen-party planning and dedicated human support.
- Wants suppliers already accustomed to large occasion groups.
- Is booking in a destination where GoHen has stronger coverage.
- Prefers an established occasion-specific brand.

#### Why you

Roamly should not claim that split payments are unique. GoHen already offers individual guest payments, instalments and reminders.

Roamly’s case must rest on the quality of the experience, ease of discovery and suitability of its local supply.

A stronger product proposition is:

Find something your group will genuinely love, book it together, and let everyone manage their own share.

### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

#### Quick dismiss: common competitive claims

| GoHen claim | Roamly response |
|---|---|
| We make group payments easy. | So do we. The question is which experience your group wants to book. |
| We organise the whole weekend. | That’s a strength. Roamly is focused on discovering and booking exceptional local experiences, not necessarily the entire weekend. |
| We have suppliers who understand hen groups. | That’s important. Roamly needs to ensure its hosts are equally comfortable with the group size and occasion. |
| We have established destination coverage. | That’s a real advantage. Roamly should only compete where it has relevant local experiences and available hosts. |

#### Landmine questions

Use questions that uncover the customer’s actual problem rather than leading with product features.

1. “If you’ve already solved collecting everyone’s money, what’s still the hardest part of organising the experience?”
2. Are you looking to organise an entire weekend, or simply find one exceptional experience for the group?
3. How important is it that the activity is designed specifically for hen or stag groups?
4. Have you already decided on the destination, and how flexible are your dates?
5. What matters most: convenience, something distinctive, or having the whole weekend organised for you?

#### Objection handling

| Objection | Suggested response |
|---|---|
| “GoHen already lets everyone pay separately.” | Absolutely. Split payments are not the differentiator. Roamly is about discovering and booking the right local experience together. |
| “GoHen can organise the whole weekend.” | Yes, and if you want a complete package, they may be the better fit. Roamly is focused on the experience itself. |
| “Will your hosts accept a group of 12?” | We should confirm group capacity and suitability before recommending an experience. |
| “Do you cover our destination?” | We should check actual local availability rather than rely on the headline number of cities. |
| “Why shouldn’t we use a specialist?” | If you want a traditional hen weekend, a specialist makes sense. If you want a distinctive local experience, Roamly is worth considering. |

#### Competitive landmines to validate internally

Before scaling the segment, Roamly should establish:

- Host appetite: Will hosts accept groups of 6–12, including hen and stag groups?
- Experience suitability: Which experiences work for occasion groups, and which should be excluded?
- Destination coverage: Does Roamly have sufficient supply in the most popular occasion destinations?
- Availability: Can hosts accommodate groups on peak weekends and popular dates?
- Group economics: Are group bookings commercially attractive to hosts and Roamly?
- Fallback options: What happens when a host declines or a group changes its requirements?

### 5. Product and GTM Implications for Roamly

#### Product benchmark

GoHen’s VIP Area provides a useful competitive benchmark. Roamly should compare its group journey against:

- Guest invitations and registration.
- Individual payment tracking.
- Payment reminders.
- Instalment support.
- Shared itinerary visibility.
- Guest changes and cancellations.
- Clear handling of unpaid guest shares.

The objective is not to copy every feature, but to ensure the group experience is credible and friction-free.

#### Recommended go-to-market approach

Primary motion: Expand existing customer value.

1. Identify existing Roamly customers exhibiting multi-account or group-booking behaviour.
2. Introduce Groups to a controlled cohort.
3. Measure completed group bookings and where participants disengage.
4. Use invite acceptance, date agreement and payment events to identify funnel friction.
5. Collect targeted feedback to understand why groups drop out.
6. Broaden the segment only when host suitability and destination coverage are demonstrated.

Secondary motion: Prove guest-led acquisition.

Each group invitation creates an opportunity to reach new customers. Measure whether invited guests subsequently make independent bookings rather than assuming invitations automatically create acquisition.

#### Success measures

| Measure | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| Incremental completed group bookings | Whether Groups increases value from existing customers. |
| Booking completion rate | Whether the group journey converts. |
| Invite acceptance | Whether guests engage with the proposition. |
| Date agreement | Whether groups can coordinate successfully. |
| Guest payment completion | Whether the payment model works in practice. |
| Subsequent independent guest bookings | Whether invitations generate incremental acquisition. |
| Host acceptance and fulfilment | Whether Roamly’s supply is suitable for the segment. |
| Destination coverage | Whether Roamly can compete where demand exists. |

Protect solo booking conversion, ratings and repeat booking rates as guardrails.

⸻

### 6. CPO Verdict

GoHen is a very high competitive threat.

It already solves much of the group coordination and payment problem that Roamly Groups is designed to address. Split payments alone are not a defensible differentiator.

GoHen’s strongest advantages are:

1. Occasion-specific expertise and brand recognition.
2. Suppliers selected for hen and stag groups.
3. Established destination coverage.
4. Complete packages and human planning support.
5. Existing group payment and coordination functionality.

Roamly’s opportunity is to own a different part of the customer journey: discovering and booking distinctive local experiences together.

However, two structural questions must be answered before Roamly commits to this segment:

- Supply suitability: Will curated hosts accept and deliver good experiences for occasion groups?
- Destination coverage: Does Roamly have relevant, bookable supply in the cities where these groups actually travel?

If either is missing, better messaging or a smoother booking flow will not close the gap.

#### Final recommendation

Compete selectively, not head-on.

Target groups looking for one exceptional local experience, where Roamly has suitable hosts, availability and destination coverage.

Do not try to out-GoHen GoHen on complete weekend planning. Win on the quality and convenience of discovering and booking the experience itself.
