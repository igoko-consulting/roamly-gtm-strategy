## GPT-6

I’d give each surface a different job: the in-app notification should prompt action, the email subject should earn attention, and the social post should communicate the broader emotional benefit.

### 1. In-app notification

Copy

Planning a big birthday? Book one special experience for 6–12 people, let everyone pay their own share, and enjoy the day without chasing payments.

Leads with: The core value proposition, supported by the booking and payment capabilities.

Why: It connects an immediately recognisable occasion to the practical benefit. It also gives the user a clear reason to explore Groups without explaining every feature.

### 2. Email subject line

Copy

Your celebration, your friends, one less bill to worry about

Leads with: The emotional benefit of organising a celebration without carrying the financial burden.

Why: It speaks to the organiser’s frustration rather than sounding like a product announcement. It creates curiosity while keeping the benefit human and relevant.

### 3. Social post

Copy

Big birthday? Anniversary? Family reunion?

Find a special local experience for 6–12 people with Roamly Groups. One person books, everyone pays their own share by the deadline, and the whole group sees the same plan.

With 12,000+ vetted local hosts across Roamly, there’s plenty to discover. And the person organising gets to enjoy the celebration too, without fronting the bill or chasing friends for money.

Leads with: The target occasions, followed by the product capabilities and supporting platform evidence.

Why: Social needs to explain the proposition to people who may never have heard of Roamly. The occasions help the right audience recognise themselves, while the host network adds credibility.

⸻

### Messaging consistency check

| Surface | Lead message | Primary job |
|---|---|---|
| In-app | Enjoy the day without chasing payments | Drive discovery and action |
| Email subject | Your celebration, without the financial burden | Earn attention and opens |
| Social | A special experience everyone can enjoy and pay for individually | Build awareness and explain the offer |

One important evidence distinction: The 12,000+ vetted hosts and 3× repeat-booking rate are platform-level evidence, not proof of group-booking performance. The beta completion rate and organiser stories should only be presented as results once measured. Until then, treat them as validation targets, not marketing claims.

## Claude

### 1. In-app notification

Booking for 6 or more?
Book once for the whole group. Everyone pays their own share through a link, so you don't front the bill.

- Leads with: capability. The user is already in the app looking at an experience, so they don't need persuading on the outcome. They need to know the mechanism exists at that moment.
- Trigger: show it when the user selects a group size of 6+ or starts checkout with a group. Shown out of context, it would just be noise.

### 2. Email subject line

Book the big day, then enjoy it like everyone else
Preview text: Everyone pays their own share. No fronting the bill, no chasing.

- Leads with: value prop. In the inbox, nobody is thinking about booking yet. The emotional outcome, getting to be a guest at your own celebration, earns the open. The preview text adds the capability as proof the promise is real.

### 3. Social post

You picked the date. You found something special. Then you spent weeks chasing eleven friends for their share.
Roamly Groups sorts the last bit. Book one experience for 6–12 people with a vetted local host who's said yes to groups. Send everyone a link and each person pays their own share by the deadline. Anyone who hasn't paid? Your call: cover them or free up the place.
Pick something special. Then enjoy it with everyone else.
Hosted by locals from Roamly's 12,000+ vetted hosts.

- Leads with: value prop, framed through the pain. A social audience is cold and scrolling. Recognition ("that's me") stops the scroll faster than a feature or a statistic.
- The capability carries the middle. The deadline-and-choice rule answers the worry about dropouts before anyone raises it.
- Evidence closes it. The 12,000+ vetted hosts figure adds credibility without overclaiming. It describes the platform, and the post doesn't imply all 12,000 take groups.

### Why none of them lead with evidence

- The group proof doesn't exist yet. Completion rate and organiser stories are stage 1 targets, not results. Don't put numbers or testimonials in market until the beta produces them.
- The 3× repeat rate proves solo loyalty, not group delivery. It's a supporting signal, not a headline.
- Once beta results land, retest the social post with an organiser story as the opening line. A real organiser's quote will likely outperform the pain-led hook.
