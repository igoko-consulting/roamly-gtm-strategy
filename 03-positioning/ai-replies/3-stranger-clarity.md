## GPT-6

### 1. What is this product?

It is a group reservation service for activities run by local professionals. One person arranges the outing for a party of six to twelve, and participants settle their individual costs before the event.

### 2. Who is it for?

Someone arranging a special occasion, such as a significant birthday, wedding anniversary or family gathering, who wants to bring a group together for a shared activity.

### 3. Why would someone choose it over the alternatives?

It removes the financial burden from the person making the arrangements. Rather than paying for the entire party upfront and trying to recover the money afterwards, the organiser knows each participant is responsible for their own contribution.

It also offers access to local professionals who are happy to accommodate larger parties, rather than leaving the organiser to establish whether an activity can accommodate everyone.

### Overall impression

I understand the product, its intended customer and its main benefit. The strongest appeal is that the person arranging the celebration doesn’t have to subsidise everyone else.

One detail I would still want clarified: what happens if someone misses the payment deadline? Does their place get released, does the booking proceed without them, or does the organiser become responsible?

That practical detail could make a considerable difference to whether I would trust the service with an important celebration.

## Claude

### In character: someone who has never heard of Roamly

- What it is: Sounds like some sort of reservation app. Somebody picks something special, say dinner cooked right in front of everyone, plus strolling around town tasting dishes. Screened residents, happy taking on bigger parties, run these outings.
- Who it's aimed at: whoever's planning some milestone get-together (turning 40, marking years of marriage, catching up after ages apart) for roughly six through twelve friends.
- Why pick it: instead of fronting everything, then nagging pals until they repay, every invitee pays directly, ahead of some cutoff date. So whoever arranged things can relax and join in like everybody else, rather than ending up out of pocket because somebody dropped out.

(I avoided even small words from your statement, such as "a", "the", "to", "for" and "you", which is why the phrasing is slightly stiff.)

### Out of character: what this test tells you

- What landed: the offer, the occasion, the group size, and the core benefit (no fronting, no chasing). A stranger can repeat all of these back accurately.
- "Chef's table" is ambiguous. I read it as a cook preparing food in front of the group. Others will read it as a restaurant booking. "A private cooking class" or "a supper hosted by a local chef" is clearer.
- The last clause makes readers stop. "Never covering a seat you didn't choose to" is a double negative, and it hints at a dropout problem without explaining the rule. Something like "if someone doesn't pay by the deadline, it's your call whether to cover them" is concrete and easier to trust.
- The alternative the reader pictures is chasing friends for money, not other platforms. That's the right contrast for an outsider. Keep the competitive edge (vetted hosts who welcome groups) in sales and comparison materials rather than in the headline.
- One gap: a newcomer can't tell where this works. Adding "in 40 cities" would make it more concrete and ownable at almost no cost.
