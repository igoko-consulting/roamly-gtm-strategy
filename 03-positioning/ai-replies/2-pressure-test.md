## GPT-6

Verdict: Yes, Airbnb Experiences could use most of this word for word.

As an Airbnb Experiences product leader, I would recognise the proposition immediately. Nothing in the statement makes it unmistakably Roamly. It describes a useful group-booking experience, but not yet a distinctive competitive position.

### What makes it too generic?

| Statement | Why it isn’t ownable |
|---|---|
| “Curated local experience” | Airbnb Experiences already offers locally hosted activities, including food tours and cooking classes. |
| “For up to 12 people” | A group-size limit is a product specification, not differentiation. |
| “Each guest paying their own share” | This is potentially differentiating against standard booking flows, but needs to be demonstrated as a capability competitors lack. |
| “Everyone seeing the same plan” | Shared itineraries and booking details are familiar group-planning functionality. |
| “Big birthday, anniversary or reunion” | These are occasions, not a distinctive customer segment or unmet need. |
| “Booking something memorable in minutes” | Generic convenience and emotional benefit language. |
| “Instead of fronting the bill or chasing friends” | A genuine pain point, but one that any group-booking competitor could claim to solve. |

### What is potentially ownable to Roamly?

The strongest element is the combination of:

1. One organiser, one booking: One person selects and coordinates the experience.
2. Individual guest payments: Everyone pays their own share rather than relying on the organiser to collect money.
3. Curated local supply: The experience comes from Roamly’s vetted local hosts.

The third point matters, but only if Roamly can demonstrate that its host network offers experiences and quality that customers cannot easily find elsewhere.

The individual payment mechanism is the clearest functional differentiator in your statement, but it is not necessarily unique across the market. Airbnb could potentially develop or offer comparable functionality.

### My recommended direction

I would make the positioning more specific by leading with the group-payment mechanism and connecting it to the experience Roamly enables.

Roamly Groups brings curated local experiences and individual guest payments together in one booking, so organisers can bring up to 12 people together without paying for everyone upfront. From milestone birthdays to family reunions, everyone gets to share the experience, while the organiser gets to enjoy it too.

### Final product leader’s assessment

The original statement describes what the feature does. It doesn’t yet establish why Roamly is the better choice.

I would retain the individual-payment benefit, make the curated local experience more central, and avoid relying on generic promises such as memorable experiences or booking in minutes.

One important caveat: before claiming this as a competitive advantage, benchmark the actual guest-payment journey against Airbnb Experiences and GetYourGuide. A feature is only a differentiator if the alternatives genuinely don’t offer an equivalent solution.

My test: If Airbnb can adopt your positioning without changing its product, it isn’t distinctive enough yet.

## Claude

### In character: Airbnb Experiences product leader

Yes, we could use it almost word for word, and most people wouldn't notice. Three edits would do it:

- Swap the name. "Airbnb Experiences lets one person book…" reads naturally.
- Raise the group size. We already take private groups of up to 30, so "up to 12" would actually undersell us.
- Keep the examples. Food tours and cooking classes are some of our best-selling categories.

The only phrase I'd hesitate over is "each guest paying their own share". That's a feature, not a moat. If it starts winning bookings, it goes on our roadmap, and we have the distribution to ship it to millions of users who already have our app.

### What makes it generic

- "Curated local experience" is the category's default claim. We say it, GetYourGuide says it, and GoHen says it about boat parties.
- "Food tour or cooking class" are examples anyone in the category could use, with nothing specific to Roamly's hosts or cities.
- "In minutes" is an assumed standard for any self-serve booking platform, not a differentiator.
- "Big birthday, anniversary or reunion" names an occasion, not an advantage. Anyone can target it.
- "Seeing the same plan": we launched a shared itinerary, so this is now parity.
- No proof. Nothing in it is something only Roamly can demonstrate.

### Stepping out of character: what would make it ownable

Anchor the statement in assets or promises Airbnb can't easily copy or won't make:

- Hosts who've opted in to groups. Turn your "Despite" into a strength. Something like "every Groups experience is run by a vetted local host who has said yes to groups of 6–12". Airbnb can't promise that across mass aggregated supply.
- A financial promise the organiser can rely on. Once your unpaid-share rule is set, say it plainly, for example "you never pay for a seat you didn't agree to cover". That's specific, testable and harder for a large platform to commit to quickly.
- Proof from your base. Your repeat rate of 3x the industry average and your vetting standard are evidence. Use one of them.
- Focus over breadth. "Built for the group", not "also does groups". Airbnb's experiences sit inside a stays-led app; yours is the core product.

### One ownable rewrite to test

Roamly Groups lets one person book an experience with a vetted local host who welcomes groups, such as a chef’s table or a private food tour, for 6–12 people, with each guest paying their own share by a set deadline. For anyone organising a big birthday, anniversary or reunion, that means booking something memorable in minutes, then enjoying it as a guest, never covering a seat you didn’t choose to.

The test before using it: confirm that host opt-in and the payment rule are true at launch. Ownable claims only work if you can back them up.
