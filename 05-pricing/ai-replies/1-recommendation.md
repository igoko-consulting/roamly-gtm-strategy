### Roamly Groups: Pricing Strategy & Recommendation

Audience: Finance, Sales Leadership & Product Marketing
Decision: Approve a transparent, per-person transaction-based pricing model, with individual guest payments and an explicit unpaid-seat policy.

#### Executive recommendation

Roamly Groups should monetise completed group bookings through its existing experience commission model, charging each guest their share of the experience price.

Do not introduce a Groups subscription, platform surcharge or additional booking fee.

The strategic opportunity is not to compete with GoHen on payment splitting. GoHen already offers that functionality to hen and stag organisers.

Roamly should win a different transaction: milestone celebrations where someone wants to book one distinctive local experience for 6–12 people, without paying for everyone upfront.

The pricing proposition should reinforce three things:

1. One standout experience: The customer pays for a curated experience with a vetted local host, not access to group-booking functionality.
2. Shared financial commitment: Each guest pays their own share, removing the need for the organiser to front the bill.
3. Organiser control: Unpaid guests are handled through an explicit deadline, after which the organiser chooses to cover the outstanding share or release the place.

My recommendation is to launch with one straightforward pricing architecture, validate willingness to pay using actual booking behaviour, and introduce premium experience options only when customer demand and host economics justify them.

#### 1. Pricing strategy: Monetise the experience, not the coordination

##### Strategic context

Roamly Groups targets organisers of milestone celebrations: big birthdays, anniversaries and reunions.

The customer wants to book one memorable experience for 6–12 people, without carrying the financial burden of organising everyone.

The strategic positioning is:

> Roamly Groups books your whole group, 6–12 people, into an experience with a vetted local host who welcomes groups, and everyone pays their own share.

This is materially different from competing on split payments alone.

GoHen already offers individual payments, payment tracking and a no-chasing proposition. Its strength is packaged hen weekends, supported by human planners and financial protection.

Roamly’s differentiation is its curated local experience inventory, self-service booking, broader occasion relevance and ability to add a standout experience to an existing trip.

Pricing must reinforce that differentiation.

##### Recommended commercial model

| Component | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue model | Existing Roamly commission on completed paid seats |
| Customer pricing | Transparent per-person experience price |
| Group size | 6–12 people |
| Payment | Each guest pays their own share through an individual link |
| Booking fee | No additional Groups fee |
| Subscription | None |
| Organiser payment | Organiser pays their own share and any additional places they voluntarily cover |
| Unpaid guests | Organiser chooses to cover or release the place at the payment deadline |
| Host compensation | Existing host commission arrangement, subject to group-specific economics |

##### Why this model?

First, it reduces adoption friction.

The product exists to remove friction from group coordination. Charging an additional fee for using Groups would introduce another objection at precisely the moment Roamly wants to encourage conversion.

Second, it aligns revenue with value creation.

Roamly earns when additional guests complete payment and attend the experience.

Third, it supports the strategic growth loop.

Every guest becomes a potential future Roamly customer. The guest experience should therefore be treated as an acquisition opportunity, not simply a payment collection mechanism.

Fourth, it avoids competing on the wrong dimension.

GoHen already demonstrates that individual payments are valuable to organisers. Roamly should use this capability to unlock demand for distinctive local experiences rather than attempt to monetise the payment functionality separately.

#### 2. Pricing architecture: Start with one proposition

I recommend against introducing Good, Better and Best tiers at launch.

The current positioning is built around one clear job: booking one standout experience for a milestone celebration.

Introducing three experience tiers would create several problems:

- It introduces distinctions between shared, private and customised experiences that have not yet been established as validated customer needs.
- It risks positioning Roamly as a generic occasion-package provider.
- It adds operational complexity for hosts who have different group capacities and availability.
- It creates an additional decision for an organiser who primarily wants to find and book something special.

Instead, use a single pricing architecture with optional experience-led upgrades where they already exist in the catalogue.

##### Recommended structure

| Element | Pricing approach |
| --- | --- |
| Standard experience | Existing per-person catalogue price |
| Group booking | Same underlying experience price, subject to group-specific host economics |
| Premium experiences | Higher price where the experience itself justifies it |
| Additional services | Separately priced only where there is demonstrable incremental value |
| Payment coordination | Included in Groups |

The customer should see the total group cost and individual share before committing.

For example:

A cooking experience priced at £65 per person for eight guests:

- Total booking value: £520.
- Organiser’s share: £65.
- Each of seven invited guests: £65.
- Roamly earns its standard commission on completed paid seats.

These figures illustrate the pricing mechanics, not a proposed new price point.

##### Should Roamly offer private experiences?

Potentially, yes.

Privacy can be valuable for milestone celebrations, but it should be treated as an attribute of an experience, not automatically as a separate pricing tier.

Where a host offers an exclusive session, Roamly should establish whether the additional price reflects:

- Lost revenue from other potential participants.
- Additional host preparation or delivery costs.
- Exclusivity that customers demonstrably value.

Only then should Roamly establish a premium.

#### 3. Stakeholder recommendations

##### Finance: Protect contribution margin and validate incremental revenue

Core message: Groups should generate incremental revenue from completed bookings without undermining existing experience economics.

Finance should evaluate four areas.

1. Contribution margin

Calculate contribution per group booking, accounting for:

- Host commission.
- Payment processing costs.
- Refunds and cancellations.
- Payment failures.
- Customer support costs.
- Any additional operational costs associated with group bookings.

2. Minimum viable group economics

Confirm that hosts can profitably accommodate groups of 6–12.

Some experiences may require minimum group commitments or different commercial arrangements.

3. Incremental revenue

Measure revenue from group bookings against comparable existing customer behaviour.

Do not count the entire group booking value as incremental revenue if some guests would otherwise have booked individually.

4. Unpaid-seat exposure

The organiser’s choice to cover or release an unpaid seat should be reflected in the financial model.

Roamly should not assume it can recover unpaid guest commitments.

Decision required: Approve the transaction-based model, subject to validated contribution margins and an agreed unpaid-seat policy.

##### Sales Leadership: Sell the experience, not the payment feature

Core message: Roamly is selling a memorable experience that the organiser can enjoy too.

The sales proposition should lead with:

- One standout experience for 6–12 people.
- A vetted local host who welcomes groups.
- Self-service booking.
- Everyone pays their own share.
- The organiser doesn’t have to front the bill.

The distinction against GoHen is important.

GoHen is the better fit for someone organising an entire hen weekend with accommodation, activities and nightlife.

Roamly is the better fit for someone who has already organised the trip, or simply wants one distinctive experience for a milestone celebration.

Sales should not claim Roamly is better at payment splitting, financial protection or human support.

Decision required: Align sales messaging with the milestone-celebration segment and ensure group-capable host inventory exists in target cities.

##### Product Marketing: Communicate value before price

Core message: Customers are paying for an experience worth sharing, with the financial coordination handled simply.

The organiser and guest require different messaging.

| Audience | Value proposition | Pricing communication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organiser | Pick something special and enjoy it with everyone else, without fronting the bill or chasing anyone. | Show total cost, individual share and payment deadline upfront. |
| Guest | Say yes, pay your share in a minute and just turn up. | Show their exact share, what’s included and the deadline. |

The product should communicate:

- What the experience includes.
- Who the host is.
- What each guest pays.
- When payment is due.
- What happens if someone doesn’t pay.
- What everyone gets back if the booking falls through.

The last point is particularly important.

GoHen benefits from ABTA protection. Roamly cannot imply equivalent protection without having it.

A clear, visible Roamly payment and cancellation guarantee is therefore a commercial priority, not merely a customer-service detail.

Decision required: Approve pricing communication and the payment/cancellation terms before launch.

#### 4. Competitive pricing: Don’t enter a price war with GoHen

GoHen is the primary competitor identified in the competitive assessment.

Its observed pricing ranges from £59 per person for a Liverpool package to £229 for HenFest, with a £25 organiser deposit shown on its homepage.

These are package prices, not directly comparable experience prices.

GoHen also offers individual guest payments and payment tracking.

Roamly should therefore avoid presenting split payments as a unique differentiator or attempting to compete on deposit size alone.

| Dimension | GoHen | Roamly Groups |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary occasion | Hen and stag weekends | Birthdays, anniversaries and reunions |
| Product | Weekend packages | One standout local experience |
| Inventory | Party-oriented suppliers | Vetted local experts |
| Booking | Quote-led, planner-supported | Self-service |
| Payment | Individual guest payments | Individual guest payments |
| Differentiation | Complete weekend, protection and human support | Curated experiences, speed and wider occasion relevance |
| Customer relationship | Primarily event-based | Potential for ongoing Roamly engagement |

The competitive implication is straightforward:

Roamly should price against the perceived value of the experience, not against the mechanics of organising a group.

It should also avoid comparing its per-person experience price with a GoHen package without adjusting for what is included.

#### 5. Critical assumptions and launch validation

Three assumptions determine whether the pricing model succeeds.

| Assumption | Why it matters | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organisers will pay the existing experience price for a group of 6–12 | Establishes whether the product unlocks demand without requiring discounting | Actual booking conversion |
| Guests will independently pay their share by the deadline | Determines whether the payment model reduces abandonment | Invite acceptance and payment completion |
| Hosts will accept group bookings at viable economics | Determines whether Roamly can reliably fulfil demand | Host acceptance, availability and contribution margin |

##### Launch measurement

During the staged launch, measure:

Commercial performance

- Completed group bookings.
- Average booking value.
- Paid seats per booking.
- Revenue and contribution margin.
- Incremental revenue versus eligible existing organisers.

Conversion

- Group checkout initiation.
- Invite acceptance.
- Guest payment completion.
- Time from invitation to payment.
- Completed bookings versus the multi-account baseline.

Supply

- Host acceptance rate.
- Availability for groups of 6–12.
- Cancellation and fulfilment rates.
- Host economics.

Growth

- Guest-to-own-booking conversion.
- Subsequent individual bookings.
- Repeat booking behaviour.

The primary success measure should be:

> Incremental completed group bookings per eligible existing organiser.

Payment completion alone is insufficient. The objective is to convert demand that would otherwise have dropped out.

Solo conversion and repeat booking rates should remain guardrails.

#### 6. Pricing research: Use behavioural evidence before sophisticated modelling

I would not make Conjoint the first research investment.

The immediate question is not which combination of hypothetical features customers prefer.

It is whether Roamly’s existing experience prices work for group organisers when the financial and coordination burden is removed.

I recommend three stages.

##### Stage 1: Qualitative price discovery

Interview organisers of milestone celebrations.

Understand:

- What they currently spend per person.
- Whether they have a total group budget.
- How they decide what an experience is worth.
- Whether they would pay the existing catalogue price.
- Whether they expect the organiser or guests to cover the honoured guest.

##### Stage 2: Behavioural price testing

Use the staged launch to measure actual conversion at existing catalogue prices.

Where volume permits, test different price points through controlled experiments, ensuring comparable experiences, occasions and group sizes.

Measure completed bookings and contribution margin, not just stated willingness to pay.

##### Stage 3: Conjoint, if the proposition expands

Conjoint becomes useful when Roamly has evidence for multiple meaningful experience attributes, such as:

- Standard versus exclusive sessions.
- Additional personalisation.
- Occasion-specific extras.
- Flexible cancellation or date changes.

At that point, conjoint can help quantify willingness to pay for combinations of experience attributes.

Van Westendorp could help establish acceptable price boundaries, but it would not establish which experience attributes generate incremental value.

#### 7. Outstanding commercial decisions

Before scaling, Roamly needs to resolve four operational questions.

| Decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Unpaid guests | Organiser chooses to cover or release the seat at a clearly communicated deadline. |
| Honoured guest | Explicitly define how the birthday person or guest of honour is paid for. Do not assume the current model covers a free place. |
| Booking cancellation | Publish a clear payment and refund guarantee before anyone pays. |
| Host commitment | Confirm group capacity and host acceptance before presenting an experience as bookable for the requested group. |

The honoured guest issue deserves particular attention.

A group of eight celebrating a birthday may expect seven people to share the cost of all eight places.

That is different from each person simply paying their own share.

Roamly should establish whether this is a common requirement and, if so, provide an explicit mechanism for distributing that cost.

It should not silently assume that the organiser will absorb it.

#### Final recommendation

Approve the following:

1. Pricing model: Existing commission-based monetisation on completed paid seats.
2. Customer pricing: Transparent per-person experience pricing, with no additional Groups fee.
3. Commercial proposition: One standout experience for 6–12 people, with everyone paying their own share.
4. Competitive positioning: Compete on curated local experiences, self-service booking and broader occasion relevance, not payment splitting.
5. Launch approach: Validate existing catalogue prices through actual group-booking behaviour before introducing premium pricing.
6. Investment priorities: Host availability, unpaid-seat rules and a clear payment/cancellation guarantee.

The central strategic principle is:

> Don’t charge customers extra for solving the coordination problem. Make money by helping them complete the experience booking they couldn’t previously organise.

#### Recommendation

- No fee for Groups. Roamly earns its standard commission on every seat in a group booking. Revenue grows from more completed seats and a higher price per seat, not from a new charge.
- Three tiers, priced per head, with Private session as the default. It fits the milestone-celebration bet and carries a premium of about 25%.
- Target audience: users we already have. Groups who currently book across separate accounts and drop off before paying (goal: Expand). Group completion rate is the north star; solo conversion, host cancellations and support load are the guardrails.
- One assumption to prove before launch: milestone groups will pay about 25% more a head to have the host to themselves.

#### Pricing at a glance

| Tier | For | Price a head | Includes | Job in the line-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Join a session | Groups of 6–12 happy to share a scheduled session | £40–£70 (host's usual price) | Seats in a scheduled session | Budget entry point |
| Private session ★ | Milestone celebrations of 6–12 | £55–£90 (about +25%) | The host to themselves | Default choice and main revenue lever |
| Private celebration | The biggest milestones | £110–£150 | Private session built around the occasion (toast, photos, chosen menu or route), plus one free date change | Premium tier and price anchor |

#### Who pays, and for what

- Guests each pay their own share through a link, by a deadline.
- Organiser pays their own place, plus any unpaid place they choose to cover.
- Honoured guest pays nothing by default. Their share is split across the paying guests.
- Hosts pay the standard commission on more seats.

Watch-out: guests pay more than the tier price. Splitting the honoured guest's share adds about 14% a head for a group of 8 and 20% for a group of 6. Take a host whose usual price is £56: a guest at an 8-person Private session pays about £80, roughly 43% above that usual price. That £80 is the number to test.

#### Finance

What you most need to hear: there is no new revenue line. Upside comes from recovering groups we lose today, at a higher value per booking, and Roamly takes no new payment risk if the unpaid-place rule is approved.

- Revenue formula: completed group bookings × seats × price a head × standard commission.
- Value per booking (8 people, illustrative): £448 at Join (£56 a head), £560 at Private session (£70) and £1,040 at Private celebration (£130). Commission scales with each.
- Two levers:
  - Recovered bookings: groups that drop off today across separate accounts.
  - Tier mix: the share of organisers who choose Private session or above.
- Risk exposure: the organiser covers or releases unpaid places, so Roamly never carries a guest's share. This depends on payments, risk and legal signing off the unpaid-place rule.
- Cost to price in: the free date change in Private celebration. Cap it at one per booking, set a notice window, and agree in advance who absorbs host costs.
- No forecast yet: don't book revenue until stage 1 beta measures completion against the multi-account baseline.
- Ask: approve the no-fee model for stage 1, and agree how the beta revenue results will be read.

#### Sales Leadership

What you most need to hear: hosts pay no new fee and get more paid seats, a private-session premium and confirmed numbers at the deadline. Supply of hosts who have opted in to groups is the constraint, so recruit for it now.

- Host pitch:
  - Fuller sessions.
  - About 25% more per head for private sessions.
  - Every seat paid or released by the deadline, never left open.
- Supply is the risk: not every host takes groups of 6–12. In each launch city, sign up hosts for private sessions with groups before the beta opens.
- Host controls: let hosts set a minimum group size or minimum price for private sessions, as Airbnb does for private groups. This protects hosts from losing a scheduled session to a small private group.
- Private celebration: sell it only to hosts who can reliably deliver the toast, photos and chosen menu. If they can't, the premium tier falls apart.
- No discounting at launch: the beta needs clean price signals.

#### Product Marketing

What you most need to hear: sell Private session as the obvious choice, and always show what each guest will actually pay.

- Tier presentation: show Private session first and mark it as the pick. Private celebration sits beside it as the anchor, and Join a session is the budget option.
- Show the real per-guest price, including the honoured guest's split, for example "Sam's share is split across the group: £80 each". A surprise at the payment link causes exactly the drop-off we're trying to fix.
- Stay on the pillar: pick something special, then enjoy it like everyone else, without fronting the bill or chasing anyone. Never say "fee", because there isn't one.
- Competitive frame: GoHen packages run from £59 to £229 a head, so our £55–£150 range fits the category. Our edge is curated local hosts booked self-serve, with each guest paying their own share. Check Airbnb's single-payer flow before making any comparison claim.
- No group proof yet: completion rates and organiser stories are beta targets, not results. Keep them out of market until the beta produces them.

#### The assumption and how to test it

Assumption: guests at a milestone celebration will pay about 25% more a head for a private session, so most organisers choose Private session over joining a scheduled one.

Recommended method: choice-based Conjoint.

- Why it fits: the assumption is a choice between tiers at different prices. Conjoint shows organisers realistic trade-offs (private or shared, occasion extras, date flexibility, price per head) and predicts what share of them would pick Private session at +15%, +25% and +35%. It also prices each element of Private celebration.
- Why not Van Westendorp: it gives an acceptable price range for a single offer. It can't tell us which tier organisers would pick, or whether privacy is worth the premium.
- Why not Max-Diff: it ranks what matters to organisers, but with no price in the design, it can't test the 25%.
- Design notes:
  - Recruit organisers of recent milestone celebrations, plus multi-account bookers from our own base.
  - Show the per-guest price after the honoured guest's split, not the tier price.
  - Confirm the result with actual tier take-up in the stage 1 beta.
