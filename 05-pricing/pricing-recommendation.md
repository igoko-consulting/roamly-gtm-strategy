# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Grow revenue from a known audience: existing users who already book in groups across separate accounts and drop off before paying, matching my Module 1 goal (Expand) and my Module 2 bet on milestone celebrations.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based: Roamly earns its standard commission on every seat in a group booking, with no separate fee for using Groups.
- **Who pays, and for what:** Each guest pays their own share of the per-person price through a link; the organiser pays only their own place and any unpaid place they choose to cover; the honoured guest's share is split across the paying guests by default, and the invite shows each guest's real share and why before anyone pays, with the organiser free to cover it instead; hosts pay the standard commission on more seats.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:** Good: Join a session, for groups of 6–12 happy to share a host's scheduled session, at the host's usual price (about £40–£70 a head); Better ★: Private session, for milestone celebrations of 6–12 who want the host to themselves, about 25% above the usual price (about £55–£90 a head); Best: Private celebration, a private session built around the occasion (a toast, photos, a menu or route the group chooses) plus one free date change, for the biggest milestones (about £110–£150 a head).
- **Launch order:** Stage 1 offers Join a session and Private session; Private celebration follows once hosts can deliver it reliably and the private premium has been tested.

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** Guests at a milestone celebration will pay about 25% more a head for a private session, even with the honoured guest's share split across them (about £80 each against a £56 usual price for a group of 8), so most organisers choose Private session over joining a scheduled one.

## 5. One-page recommendation

**Recommendation:** charge no fee for Groups. Roamly earns its standard commission on every seat, so revenue grows from recovered group bookings and a higher price a head, not from a new charge. Private session is the default tier, and the one thing to prove before launch is that guests will pay its premium once the honoured guest's share is split across them.

For Finance, Sales Leadership and Product Marketing · 18 Nov 2026

### In brief

- **No Groups fee:** Roamly earns its standard commission on every seat in a group booking. Revenue grows from more completed seats and a higher price per seat.
- **Three tiers, priced a head, with Private session as the default:** it fits the milestone-celebration bet. Stage 1 launches Join a session and Private session; Private celebration follows once hosts can deliver it.
- **Audience: users Roamly already has:** groups who book across separate accounts today and drop off before paying (goal: Expand). Group booking completion rate is the north star; solo conversion, host cancellations and support contacts are the guardrails.
- **To prove before launch:** guests will pay about 25% more a head for a private session, even at about £80 each once the honoured guest's share is split.

### Tiers at a glance

| Tier | For | Price a head | Includes | Job in the line-up | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Join a session | Groups of 6–12 happy to share a scheduled session | £40–£70 (the host's usual price) | Seats in a scheduled session | Budget entry point | 1 |

### What each guest actually pays

- **Guests:** each pays their own share through a link, by a deadline.
- **Organiser:** pays their own place, plus any unpaid place they choose to cover.
- **Honoured guest:** pays nothing by default. Their share is split across the paying guests, and the invite says so before anyone pays ("Sam's share is split across the group: £80 each"). The organiser can cover it instead.
- **Hosts:** pay the standard commission on more seats.
- **The split raises the real price:** it adds about 14% a head for a group of 8 and 20% for a group of 6. At a £56 usual price, a guest at an 8-person Private session pays about £80, around 43% above the usual price. That £80 is the figure to test, and the figure to show.

### Finance

**What Finance needs to hear:** there is no new revenue line. The upside is recovered group bookings at a higher value per booking, and Roamly carries no guest's unpaid share once the unpaid-share rule is signed off.

- **Revenue:** completed group bookings × seats × price a head × standard commission.
- **Value per booking (8 people, illustrative):** £448 at Join a session (£56 a head), £560 at Private session (£70) and £1,040 at Private celebration (£130). Commission scales with each.
- **Two levers:** recovered bookings, the groups that drop off today across separate accounts; and tier mix, the share of organisers who choose Private session or above.
- **Count only what's incremental:** some groups already complete across separate accounts. Credit Groups with the lift over the holdout, not the full value of every group booking.
- **Risk:** the organiser covers or releases unpaid places, so Roamly never carries a guest's share. Payments, risk and legal sign off the unpaid-share rule at stage 0.
- **Costs to price in:** payment processing, refunds under the guarantee, and the free date change in Private celebration, capped at one per booking with a notice window and an agreed rule on who absorbs the host's costs.
- **No forecast yet:** book no revenue until the stage 1 beta measures completion against the multi-account baseline.
- **Ask:** approve the no-fee model for stage 1, and agree how the beta revenue results will be read.

### Sales Leadership

Roamly Groups has no sales team, so sales here means host supply: the hosts who decide whether there is anything private to book.

**What Sales Leadership needs to hear:** hosts pay no new fee and get more paid seats, a private-session premium and confirmed numbers at the deadline. Hosts who take groups are the constraint, so recruit them now.

- **Host pitch:** fuller sessions; about 25% more a head for private sessions; every place paid or released by the deadline, never left open.
- **Supply is the risk:** not every host takes groups of 6–12. Sign up hosts for private group sessions in each stage 1 city before the beta opens.
- **Host controls:** let hosts set a minimum price for private sessions, as [Airbnb lets its hosts do for private groups](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/help/article/2595) (checked 18 Nov 2026), so a small private group never costs a host a full scheduled session.
- **Private celebration waits:** offer it only through hosts who can reliably deliver the toast, photos and chosen menu. If they can't, the premium tier falls apart.
- **No discounting at launch:** the beta needs clean price signals.

### Product Marketing

**What Product Marketing needs to hear:** sell Private session as the obvious choice, and always show what each guest will actually pay.

- **Tier presentation:** show Private session first, marked as the pick. Join a session is the budget option; Private celebration, once live, sits beside Private session as the anchor.
- **Show the real share:** the invite and the payment link show each guest's share after the honoured guest's split. A surprise at the payment link causes the drop-off Groups exists to fix.
- **Stay on the pillar:** pick something special, then enjoy it with everyone else, without fronting the bill or chasing anyone. Never say "fee", because there isn't one.
- **Compare on what's included, not on price:** GoHen's £59–£229 a head buys a packaged weekend, so it isn't a like-for-like price. Airbnb already sells private groups, so privacy isn't the edge either; lead with hosts who welcome groups and each guest paying their own share.
- **The guarantee is commercial, not a support detail:** GoHen has ABTA protection and Roamly has no equivalent badge, so the guarantee of what everyone gets back, shown before anyone pays, carries the trust.
- **No group proof yet:** completion rates and organiser stories are beta targets, not results. Keep them out of market until the beta produces them.

### Research before launch: Conjoint

**Method:** choice-based Conjoint, after short interviews with organisers, confirmed by tier take-up in the stage 1 beta.

- **Why Conjoint:** the question is a choice between tiers at different prices. Conjoint shows organisers realistic trade-offs (private or shared, occasion extras, date flexibility, price a head) and predicts the share who pick Private session at +15%, +25% and +35%. It also prices each element of Private celebration.
- **Why not Van Westendorp:** it gives an acceptable price range for one offer, but can't say which tier organisers pick or whether privacy is worth the premium.
- **Why not Max-Diff:** it ranks what matters to organisers but has no price in the design, so it can't test the 25%.
- **Interviews first:** a handful of milestone organisers on what they spend a head, whether they budget for the group or per person, and who they expect to pay for the honoured guest. Their answers set the Conjoint's attributes and price levels.
- **Design:** recruit organisers of recent milestone celebrations plus multi-account bookers from Roamly's own users; show the price each guest pays after the honoured guest's split, not the tier price; and separate paying for privacy from paying for the celebration around it.
