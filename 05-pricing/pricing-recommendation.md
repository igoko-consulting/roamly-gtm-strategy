# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Grow revenue from a known audience: existing users who already book in groups across separate accounts and drop off before paying, matching my Module 1 goal (Expand) and my Module 2 bet on milestone celebrations.

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based: Roamly earns its standard commission on every seat in a group booking, with no separate fee for using Groups.
- **Who pays, and for what:** Each guest pays their own share of the per-person price through a link; the organiser pays only their own place and any unpaid place they choose to cover; the honoured guest's share is split across the paying guests by default and shown on the invite before anyone pays, unless the organiser covers it; hosts pay the standard commission on more seats.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:** Good: Join a session, for groups of 6–12 happy to share a host's scheduled session, at the host's usual price (about £40–£70 a head); Better ★: Private session, for milestone celebrations of 6–12 who want the host to themselves, about 25% above the usual price, with a minimum price the host sets (about £55–£90 a head); Best: Private celebration, a private session built around the occasion (a toast, photos, a menu or route the group chooses) plus one free date change, for the biggest milestones (about £110–£150 a head).
- **Launch order:** Stage 1 offers Join a session and Private session; Private celebration follows once hosts can deliver it reliably and the private premium has been tested.

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** Guests at a milestone celebration will pay about 25% more a head for a private session, even with the honoured guest's share split across them (about £80 each against a £56 usual price for a group of 8), so most organisers choose Private session over joining a scheduled one.

## 5. One-page recommendation

For Finance, Sales Leadership and Product Marketing · 18 Nov 2026

### In brief

- **Recommendation:** no Groups fee, just Roamly's standard commission on every seat; three tiers priced a head, with Private session as the default; stage 1 launches Join a session and Private session.
- **Audience:** existing users who book across separate accounts and drop off before paying (Expand). North star: group booking completion rate. Guardrails: solo conversion, host cancellations and support contacts.
- **To prove first:** guests will pay about 25% more a head for a private session, even at about £80 each after the honoured guest's split.

### Tiers at a glance

| Tier | For | Price a head | Includes | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Join a session | Groups of 6–12 happy to share a scheduled session | £40–£70, the host's usual price | Seats in a scheduled session | 1 |
| Private session ★ | Milestone celebrations of 6–12 | £55–£90, about 25% more, above a minimum the host sets | The host to themselves | 1 |
| Private celebration | The biggest milestones | £110–£150 | A private session built around the occasion (a toast, photos, a chosen menu or route) and one free date change | Once hosts can deliver it |

Prices are rough estimates: the brief gives no Roamly prices or commission rate. Hosts price within each range.

### What each guest pays

- **The honoured guest** pays nothing by default. Their share is split across the paying guests and shown on the invite ("Sam's share is split across the group: £80 each"), unless the organiser covers it. Everyone else pays as section 2 sets out.
- **The real price:** the split adds 9% a head for 12 people, 14% for 8 and 20% for 6. At a £56 usual price, an 8-person Private session costs each guest about £80, 43% above it. That £80 is the figure to test, and to show.
- **Drop-outs before the cut-off:** the guest gets their own place back; their part of the honoured guest's share stays, so no one else pays more (8-person Private session: £70 back, £10 kept). For the private tiers the cut-off is the payment deadline, when the organiser covers or releases the place; Join a session follows Roamly's standard terms, as its seats go back on sale.
- **After the cut-off:** no refund, but a free transfer: the new guest pays through a link and the first is refunded. The host is paid in full.
- **Below the host's minimum at the deadline:** the organiser covers the gap, moves the group to Join a session if one runs that day, or cancels with everyone refunded. The invite shows how many paid places the minimum needs.

### Finance

**What Finance needs to hear:** there is no new revenue line, and no new payment risk once payments, risk and legal sign off the unpaid-share rule at stage 0.

- **Revenue:** bookings × seats × price a head × standard commission. For 8 people, illustratively: £448 at Join a session (£56 a head), £560 at Private session (£70) and £1,040 at Private celebration (£130).
- **Levers:** recovered bookings and tier mix. Count only the lift over the holdout, since some groups already complete across separate accounts.
- **Costs:** payment processing, refunds under the guarantee, and Private celebration's date change, capped at one per booking with a notice window and an agreed rule on host costs.
- **Ask:** approve no fee for stage 1, book no revenue until the beta reads completion against the multi-account baseline, and agree now how to read it.

### Sales Leadership

With no sales team, sales here means host supply.

**What Sales Leadership needs to hear:** hosts who take groups are the constraint. Recruit them now: no new fee, more paid seats, about 25% more a head for private sessions, and every place paid or released by the deadline.

- **Supply:** not every host takes groups of 6–12, so sign up hosts for private group sessions in each stage 1 city before the beta opens.
- **Controls:** hosts set a minimum price for private sessions, as [Airbnb's hosts can for private groups](https://www.airbnb.co.uk/help/article/2595) (checked 18 Nov 2026), so a small group never costs them a full session.
- **Private celebration waits** for hosts who can reliably deliver its extras.
- **No discounting at launch:** the beta needs clean price signals.

### Product Marketing

**What Product Marketing needs to hear:** sell Private session as the obvious choice, and always show what each guest pays.

- **Tiers:** Private session first, marked as the pick; Join a session as the budget option; Private celebration, once live, as the anchor.
- **The real share:** show it after the split, on the invite and the payment link. A surprise there causes the drop-off Groups exists to fix.
- **Words:** stay on the Module 4 pillar, and never say "fee".
- **Edge:** GoHen's £59–£229 a head buys a packaged weekend, and Airbnb already sells private groups. Lead with hosts who welcome groups and each guest paying their own share, not with price or privacy.
- **Trust and proof:** Roamly has no badge like GoHen's ABTA protection, so the guarantee, shown before anyone pays, carries the trust. Group results stay out of market until the beta produces them.

### Research before launch: Conjoint

**Method:** choice-based Conjoint, after short interviews with organisers, confirmed by tier take-up in the stage 1 beta.

- **Why Conjoint:** the question is a choice between tiers at different prices. Conjoint trades privacy, occasion extras, date flexibility and price a head against each other, predicts the share choosing Private session at +15%, +25% and +35%, and prices Private celebration's extras. Van Westendorp prices one offer, not a choice; Max-Diff has no price in it.
- **Interviews first:** what organisers spend a head, whether they budget per group or per person, and who they expect to pay for the honoured guest. Their answers set the Conjoint's attributes and price levels.
- **Design:** recent milestone organisers plus Roamly's own multi-account bookers; each guest's price shown after the split; paying for privacy separated from paying for the celebration around it.
- **What the result decides:** if fewer than half of organisers choose Private session at about £80 a guest, Join a session becomes the default and Private session stays as an option. The no-fee model stands either way.
